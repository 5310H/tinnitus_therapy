🚀 Quick Start Guide

New to the suite? Follow these essential steps to set up your therapy environment:

  1. Narrated Guidance: Use the Tutorial button at the top of any therapy page for a step-by-step walkthrough. A professional narrator and visual highlights will guide you through the clinical setup.
  2. Run System Validation: Access System Validation from the dashboard. Execute the 'Engine Validation' and 'Phase Test' to confirm your hardware and browser settings are delivering accurate clinical signals.
  3. Establish a Baseline: Navigate to CBT & Wellness and complete the THI Assessment. This establishes your initial distress score, allowing the Stats dashboard to track your habituation progress.
  4. Check Your Hearing: Use the Hearing Test tool to map your current audibility thresholds and identify potential dead zones.
  5. Match Your Pitch: Use the Notch Finder tool to identify your unique tinnitus frequency. Saving this value automatically calibrates Notch Therapy and CR Neuromodulation for your profile.

Tip: Once these steps are finished, use the Personalized Recommendations tool to find the best therapy mode for your specific scores.

1. Stereo Architecture

The suite utilizes a Discrete Stereo Routing engine. Unlike standard audio players, our therapy modules generate independent signal chains for the Left and Right channels. This allows users to compensate for asymmetric hearing loss or ear-specific tinnitus frequencies.

  • Lock L/R: Synchronizes both channels to the "Master" Left ear settings.
  • Master Gain: A global multiplier stage that preserves your custom L/R balance while scaling overall loudness.
  • Headphone Type: For optimal decorrelation, open-back headphones are highly recommended. Closed-back headphones can sometimes create internal reflections that reduce the perceived decorrelation effect.

2. Mobile Precision Controls

Recognizing the limitations of standard sliders on touchscreens, we have implemented a multi-tier control system:

  • Sliders: For coarse, rapid frequency sweeps.
  • Fine-Tune Buttons (±1, ±5): For exact pitch matching without needing a keyboard.
  • Coarse Step Buttons (±10, ±100): For quick navigation across octaves.

3. Validation & Safety

To ensure clinical integrity, the suite includes automated diagnostic tools:

  • Hardware Phase Audit: Checks for inverted headphone wiring which can negate the effects of decorrelated noise.
  • DSP Validation: A real-time mathematical check of filter attenuation (Target: >40dB) and frequency accuracy (Target: ±1%).
  • Recalibration Cycle: The system highlights the validation tool every 30 days to ensure hardware consistency.

4. Diagnostic Mapping

The suite includes sophisticated tools to characterize the "acoustic footprint" of your tinnitus:

  • TMC: Maps the Minimum Masking Level (MML) to calculate the Q-factor, identifying the selectivity of your auditory filters.
  • LG Test: Quantifies hyperacusis by measuring how your brain scales volume, helping to set safe therapy limits.

Interpreting Q-Factor Results

The Q-factor (Quality Factor) describes the "sharpness" of your auditory filter at your tinnitus frequency. In the TMC tool, this is calculated by center frequency divided by the bandwidth at 10 units above your Minimum Masking Level.

  • High Q-Factor (>4.0): Suggests "Sharp Tuning." This is typically associated with tonal tinnitus and localized cochlear damage. It often indicates that Notch Therapy may be highly effective.
  • Low Q-Factor (<2.0): Suggests "Broad Tuning." This is common in noise-like or "hissing" tinnitus and may indicate more significant central auditory involvement. Neuromodulation or Decorrelated Noise is often more helpful here than simple notch filters.

8. Hearing Health & Audibility Testing

Regularly monitoring your hearing thresholds is a vital part of long-term tinnitus management. Because tinnitus is often linked to auditory deprivation (hearing loss), changes in your hearing can directly impact your tinnitus perception.

  • The Tinnitus-Hearing Connection: Tinnitus frequently occurs at frequencies where hearing sensitivity has decreased. The brain "turns up the gain" in these regions, which can manifest as phantom sound.
  • Why Check Regularly? Gradual changes in hearing may require you to recalibrate your EQ Boost settings or adjust your Notch parameters to maintain therapeutic efficacy.
  • Using the Hearing Range Test:

    Navigate to the Hearing Test tool. Starting with your system volume low (approx. 5-10%), tap each frequency button from 20Hz to 20kHz. Identify the highest and lowest frequencies you can reliably detect.

  • Safety & Clinical Disclaimer:

    Always perform tests at comfortable levels. This tool is intended for auditory exploration and tracking only. It is not a calibrated clinical audiogram and should not be used to diagnose medical conditions. Always consult an audiologist for professional hearing evaluations.

9. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) - Habituation Timelines

Understanding the habituation process is key to successful tinnitus management. Here are answers to common questions about how long it takes to see results:

  • How long does habituation typically take?

    Tinnitus habituation is a gradual process. Clinical studies and expert consensus suggest that significant changes in distress levels and perceived loudness typically occur over 3 to 12 months of consistent therapy use.

  • What factors influence the habituation timeline?

    Several factors can affect how quickly you habituate, including the severity and duration of your tinnitus, consistency of therapy use, and individual neuroplasticity.

  • How often should I use the therapy tools?

    Most protocols recommend daily use, typically for 30 to 60 minutes per session. Consistency is more important than intensity.

10. Troubleshooting & Error Resolution

The suite performs real-time validation of the audio signal. If you see a failure in your clinical report or the status bar, follow these steps:

"Shallow Notch" Failure

This error occurs when the DSP engine detects that the notch filter is not reducing sound energy by at least 40dB at your target frequency. Clinical research suggests that a deep notch is required to effectively trigger lateral inhibition.

  • Disable Audio Enhancers: "Volume Boosters" or "Equalizer" browser extensions often compress the audio signal, which "fills in" the notch. Disable these during therapy.
  • System Post-Processing: Turn off "Spatial Sound," "Dolby Atmos," or "Audio Enhancements" in your Windows/macOS sound settings. These algorithms interfere with precision filter slopes.
  • Sample Rate Alignment: Ensure your OS audio output is set to 44.1kHz or 48kHz. Extreme sample rate mismatches can lead to mathematical rounding errors in filter calculations.

"Frequency Mismatch" Failure

Occurs when the measured output frequency deviates by more than 1% from the target. This is typically caused by virtual surround sound software re-sampling the audio in real-time.
